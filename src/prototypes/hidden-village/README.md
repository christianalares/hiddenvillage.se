# Hidden Village walk prototype

A personal, slightly unsettling woodland journey, built in editable Blender scenes and rendered with Three.js. The existing homepage is unchanged. The prototype lives at `/prototype/hidden-village/index.html` on `codex/hidden-village-walk-prototype`.

The walk descends from the high moor through birches, a compulsory overgrown passage, a ravine bridge and the village. Three physical fingerposts offer the ridge shelter, workshop or village, and music hollow or village lane. The direct route measures approximately 287 metres; all detours together measure 515 metres. Mystery comes from fog, wind, distant animals, dark windows and a motionless silhouette, without jump scares.

## Run and export

```sh
npm install
npm run prototype:walk
```

Open the local address printed by Next.js, followed by `/prototype/hidden-village/index.html`.

The authoritative editable sources are `blender/hidden-village.blend`, `blender/signpost.blend` and `blender/deer.blend`. They were edited with Blender 5.2.2 LTS, with images packed into the files. After saving edits:

```sh
npm run prototype:assets
npm run prototype:bundle
```

The exporter reads the saved sources without reconstructing the scene. Set `BLENDER_BIN` if Blender is not installed at `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender`. Web exports use pinned `@gltf-transform/cli@4.5.1`, Meshopt geometry and WebP textures capped at 1K. Commit the `.blend` files and GLBs together. Vercel only bundles the runtime; it does not need Blender. `public/prototype/hidden-village/scene.js` is deliberately ignored and generated by the production build.

## Walking and looking

Native document scrolling controls travel speed between decisions, including trackpad momentum, touch swipes and keyboard scrolling. A choice stops forward movement while allowing backward scrolling. Escape also steps back. Click, tap or keyboard-activate a wooden arrow to travel six metres along that route, then continue walking. Returning to a fork allows a different choice. Waypoints respect unanswered decisions.

The round fingerposts have independently oriented arms aimed along the outgoing paths, with painted lettering on both sides. Hover brightens the actual timber. Invisible HTML hit areas provide touch and keyboard access; only keyboard focus has an outline. The old ungated workshop sign is removed.

Drag the scene to look around. Scrolling smoothly restores the authored gaze. Vertical touch gestures retain native walking; horizontal dragging looks around. Reduced motion starts with atmosphere paused and uses immediate inspection/route transitions.

## Letters and things to inspect

Letters are physical sheets on the tables. Opening one moves the existing camera towards it; no prose modal replaces the world. Locally hosted Kalam handwriting is drawn onto a worn paper texture. Long letters have readable pages, with a page-turn control. The view refits on resize. Escape or Step back returns to the same walking position. An accessible transcript accompanies each sheet and keyboard focus stays in the inspection controls. About Christian and Field notes remain ordinary accessible dialogs.

| Place | Discovery | Personal thread |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moor | An invitation | Christian’s little corner of the internet |
| Workshop | The missing thing | Building useful software; care for how things feel |
| Ridge | An open question, usable telescope | AI, curiosity and connections |
| Music hollow | A letter, drum kit and records | Drumming and former music production |
| Beyond the hollow | Weathered blue padel court | Padel and life away from the keyboard |
| Last light | Postcard and folded letter | Stockholm, family in broad terms, welcome |

Three marks, found by reading the workshop, ridge and music pages, unlock an optional final letter. They are stored only in this browser under `hidden-village-pages-v1`. Biography and contact are always accessible without completing the walk or collecting anything. There are no project showcases, skill lists or freelance claims.

The telescope approaches its eyepiece and magnifies the actual tower across the valley. Its housing is excluded by the close clipping plane while looking through it, and the normal view is restored on exit. Visitors can also approach the drum kit, look into the well and look across the blue court through its open side gate. The court has faded blue felt, service lines, an enclosing fence, glass rebound panels, a sagging net, fallen branches and ferns. Stone retaining walls meet the ground. Nearby old trees were cleared from the playing area.

## Sound and future voice recordings

Sound starts off. The Sound button or an explicit drum tap creates/resumes Web Audio after the visitor’s gesture. Wind, occasional distant bird-like calls and the drum sound are locally synthesized, with no downloaded recordings or external requests. Mute suspends the audio context. Hidden tabs suspend audio and pause narration. Atmosphere motion and sound have separate controls.

Every letter has a Listen control, clearly marked “coming soon” while its recording is absent. No synthetic voice stands in for Christian. To add recordings, put the audio files under `public/prototype/hidden-village/assets/audio/` and set the corresponding `pages[id].audio` URL in `letters.ts`. The six IDs are `invitation`, `making`, `curiosity`, `rhythm`, `home` and `letter`. Playback starts from the Listen click, handles rejected playback, lowers ambient volume during narration and stops on leaving the sheet.

A normal playback gesture is the relevant browser requirement here, not microphone access. The implementation follows [Chrome’s autoplay guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay/). No permission prompt or microphone request is added.

## Runtime and performance

The compressed woodland is approximately 8.7 MB, the deer 403 KB and the articulated fingerpost 244 KB. Existing scanned woodland assets and textures are reused. No external asset service is contacted during a visit. Desktop viewports additionally load the existing 2K foreground-rock map; phones keep its embedded 1K version.

Shared vegetation and rocks use instanced batches in 48-metre cells. Architecture and small solids merge by material/cell; missing UV attributes are normalized before merging. Ferns, roots, rocks and trees have distance culling. Deer outside the view are culled and not animated. Visible nearby deer receive a 30 FPS render budget, instead of the old 10 FPS idle update. Other idle atmosphere is capped at 20 FPS; movement and camera transitions at 30 FPS. These are scheduling caps, not guaranteed device frame rates.

Only two nearby point lights illuminate geometry. Emissive lantern panes and baked ground pools remain lit at a distance. No shadows, reflections, bloom or other extra render passes were introduced. DPR is capped at 1 on phones and 1.25 on desktop. Wind runs in vegetation shaders; leaves and mist each use a single draw call. Notes stop rendering once the camera settles and redraw for page changes. Pause atmosphere stops rendering at rest. Hidden tabs stop rendering. Camera far clipping stays at 150 metres except while using the telescope.

Canvas data attributes expose camera position, route state, inspection, look offsets, draw calls, triangles and frame count. `renderMs` measures CPU submission only, not GPU time or physical-device FPS. Physical phone thermals and battery consumption remain hardware checks.

## Verification and authoring notes

```sh
npm run check:types
npm run lint
npm run prototype:bundle
npx esbuild src/prototypes/hidden-village/journey.test.ts --bundle --platform=node --outfile=/tmp/hidden-village-journey.test.cjs
node --test /tmp/hidden-village-journey.test.cjs
```

Journey tests cover forward gating, backward retreat, all three choices, six-metre departures, rearming, scroll-distance round trips and browser pixel rounding. `blender/check_routes.py` verifies sampled camera points against the actual saved terrain and obstacles. The current pass checks eight route combinations at half-metre intervals. Browser captures and measurements are in `qa/`. A local full Next.js production build requires the homepage’s existing Notion credentials; deployment is verified against the exact pushed commit on Vercel.

Blender uses Z-up. Authoring helpers map Three.js `(x, y, z)` to Blender `(x, -z, y)`. Terrain/route equations are in `trail.ts` and `blender/landscape.py`; keep them in agreement. Extra properties `discovery`, `inspect`, `eye`, `target`, `lamp`, `animal` and `motion` preserve interaction anchors and moving hierarchies during export. Wind and animation remain runtime concerns.

Construction scripts are records, not an idempotent replacement for exporting the saved source. Earlier history includes `build_scene.py`, `build_discoveries.py` and the subsequent discovery refinements. This pass adds `deepen_woodland.py`, `refine_mystery.py` and `refine_court.py`, in that order. The first preserves a local `.cache/pre-mystery.blend` backup. Reconstruct only on a copy. `build_signpost.py` independently rebuilds the fingerpost. Source downloads, checksums and licenses are in `blender/sources.json`, `fetch_assets.py` and [ATTRIBUTION.md](ATTRIBUTION.md).
